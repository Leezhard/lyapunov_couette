# 2. The critical mode, the symmetries, and why `E` is the wrong functional

## 2.1 Symmetries of the problem

The perturbation equations about `U = (y, 0, 0)` on `y ∈ [−1,1]` are invariant
under the group generated by

| symmetry | action |
|---|---|
| streamwise translation `T_x^a` | `(x,y,z) → (x+a, y, z)` |
| spanwise translation `T_z^c` | `(x,y,z) → (x, y, z+c)` |
| spanwise reflection `R_z` | `(x,y,z) → (x,y,−z)`, `(u,v,w) → (u,v,−w)` |
| centro-rotation `R_c` | `(x,y,z) → (−x,−y,z)`, `(u,v,w) → (−u,−v,w)` |

`R_c` is the one that encodes the oddness of the base flow: `U(−y) = −U(y)`.
Its presence means the *quadratic forms in (1.2) are `R_c`-invariant*, so the
Reynolds–Orr eigenspaces split into `R_c`-even and `R_c`-odd subspaces, and any
optimal weight `Σ(y)` may be taken **even in `y`** (if `Σ` is admissible so is
its reflection, and the eigenvalue functional is convex in `Σ` — §3.6).

Translation invariance in `x` and `z` is what makes the Fourier decoupling of
§1.4 exact; `R_z` means `Re_E(α, β) = Re_E(α, −β)`, so the scan may be
restricted to `β ≥ 0`.

## 2.2 The critical mode

`scripts/02_critical_mode.py`, at `(α_c, β_c) = (0, 1.5581617774)`:

**Parities about the centreline.** `u` even, `v` even, `w` odd. (This is the
`R_c`-even branch: under `y → −y` with `(u,v) → (−u,−v)` the mode maps to
itself up to the overall sign.)

**Energy split** (unit total energy):

| component | share |
|---|---|
| `u` (streak) | 0.6615 |
| `v` (roll, wall-normal) | 0.1518 |
| `w` (roll, spanwise) | 0.1867 |
| roll total `(v,w)` | 0.3385 |

with `max|u| / max|v| = 1.935`.

The physical structure is the classical **roll–streak**: a pair of
counter-rotating streamwise vortices, `(0, v, w)`, of spanwise wavelength
`2π/β_c ≈ 4.03` half-gaps, which by the lift-up mechanism generates a much
larger streamwise streak `u`. Two-thirds of the critical mode's energy sits in
the streak, but the streak is *not* what makes the energy grow — the production
`−∫ u v` needs both.

**Spectral gap.** The Reynolds–Orr spectrum at the critical wavenumbers is

```
λ₀ = 0.04839677  (1/λ₀ =   20.66)   ← critical
λ₁ = 0.01265205  (1/λ₁ =   79.04)
λ₂ = 0.00482575  (1/λ₂ =  207.22)
λ₃ = 0.00229887  (1/λ₃ =  435.00)
```

`λ₁/λ₀ = 0.261`: the dangerous direction is **strongly isolated**. This matters
directly for the construction of §3 — a *finite-rank* correction of very low
rank should suffice to control the dangerous subspace, because everything else
is separated from it by a factor of four in the Rayleigh quotient.

**Width of the dangerous cone.** `(Re_E(α,β) − Re_E)/Re_E` in percent:

| | β=1.25 | β=1.40 | β=1.5582 | β=1.70 | β=1.90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| α=0.00 | 3.30 | 0.80 | 0.00 | 0.56 | 3.03 |
| α=0.10 | 3.48 | 0.97 | 0.17 | 0.72 | 3.19 |
| α=0.20 | 4.02 | 1.49 | 0.67 | 1.21 | 3.66 |
| α=0.35 | 5.50 | 2.92 | 2.04 | 2.54 | 4.94 |
| α=0.50 | 7.78 | 5.11 | 4.16 | 4.60 | 6.94 |

At `Re = Re_E(1 + ε)` the set of directions with `dE/dt > 0` is confined to the
region where this table is below `100ε` percent. For `ε = 0.2%` (i.e.
`Re = 20.70`) that is a *very* thin neighbourhood of `(0, 1.558)` — only modes
with `α ≲ 0.1` and `β ∈ (1.5, 1.62)` participate at all.

## 2.3 The decisive structural fact: the `α = 0` subspace is globally stable at **every** `Re`

The energy method fails first on a streamwise-independent disturbance. But
streamwise-independent disturbances of plane Couette flow are unconditionally
globally stable — for all `Re`, at all amplitudes. The proof is three lines.

Let `∂_x ≡ 0` throughout.

**(i) The rolls decouple and obey the 2D Navier–Stokes equations.** Continuity
reduces to `∂_y v + ∂_z w = 0`, so `(v,w)` is a divergence-free 2D field in the
`(y,z)` plane. The pressure Poisson equation is `Δp = −2∂_x v − ∂_j u_i ∂_i u_j`;
with `∂_x ≡ 0` every term containing an index `1` vanishes, so `p` is
`x`-independent and determined by `(v,w)` alone. The `y`- and `z`-momentum
equations contain no `u` and no base-flow term (the base flow enters only
through `y ∂_x` and `−v e_x`, both of which live in the `u` equation). Hence
`(v,w)` satisfies the 2D Navier–Stokes equations *exactly*, with no forcing.

**(ii) Therefore the rolls decay, unconditionally.** For 2D Navier–Stokes with
no forcing,

```
d/dt ( ½‖(v,w)‖² ) = − Re⁻¹ ‖∇(v,w)‖² ≤ − (λ₁/Re) ‖(v,w)‖²,
```

`λ₁` being the smallest eigenvalue of `−Δ` on the admissible space. So
`‖(v,w)‖(t) ≤ ‖(v,w)‖(0) e^{−λ₁ t/Re}` for every `Re` and every amplitude.

**(iii) The streak then decays too.** With `∂_x p = 0` the `u`-equation is
`∂_t u + (v∂_y + w∂_z)u = −v + Re⁻¹ Δu`, and the advection term is
energy-neutral, so

```
d/dt ( ½‖u‖² ) = − ∫ u v dV − Re⁻¹‖∇u‖²   ⟹   d‖u‖/dt ≤ ‖v‖ − (λ₁/Re)‖u‖.
```

Since `‖v‖ → 0` exponentially, so does `‖u‖`. ∎

**Consequence.** At `Re = Re_E + ε` the energy functional reports growth in a
cone of directions that lies (to leading order in `ε`) *inside a subspace on
which the true dynamics is provably, globally, unconditionally stable*. The
energy method is not detecting an instability; it is detecting **transient
growth** — the lift-up mechanism, which converts a decaying roll into a large
streak before the streak itself decays.

This is the strongest possible encouragement for the programme of beating
`Re_E`: the obstruction at `Re_E` is an artefact of the functional, not a
property of the flow. It also says precisely what a better functional must do —
**it must weight the roll `(v,w)` more heavily than the streak `u`**, so that
the roll's decay is "paid forward" against the streak's transient growth.

## 2.4 What goes wrong if one simply reweights the components

The obvious repair is the weighted quadratic functional

```
V_μ = ½ ∫ ( u² + μ (v² + w²) ) dV,      μ > 1.
```

Its derivative is computed from the component energies. Write
`A = ½‖u‖²`, `G = ½‖(v,w)‖²`, `E = A + G`, and

```
Π := ∫ p ∂_x u dV                                                        (2.1)
```

for the pressure–streak-gradient correlation. Then

```
dA/dt = − ∫ u v dV − Re⁻¹‖∇u‖² + Π,
dG/dt =              − Re⁻¹‖∇(v,w)‖² − Π,                                (2.2)
```

(the two `Π`'s cancel in `dE/dt`, recovering (1.1), because
`∂_y v + ∂_z w = −∂_x u`). Note that the *advective nonlinearity does not
transfer energy between `A` and `G` at all* — `∫ u (u·∇)u = 0` — so **the only
exchange between streamwise and cross-stream energy is mediated by the
pressure**. Consequently

```
dV_μ/dt = − ∫ u v dV − Re⁻¹( ‖∇u‖² + μ‖∇(v,w)‖² ) + (1 − μ) Π.           (2.3)
```

Two things follow.

* **On the `α = 0` subspace the reweighting is free and unboundedly effective.**
  There `Π = 0`, and optimising the relative amplitude of streak and roll in the
  Rayleigh quotient gives exactly

  ```
  Re_E^{(μ)} |_{α = 0} = √μ · Re_E .                                      (2.4)
  ```

  (If `N = −∫uv` is the bilinear production and `D₁ = ‖∇u‖²`, `D₂ = ‖∇(v,w)‖²`,
  then scaling `u → s u` gives `max_s sN/(s²D₁ + μD₂) = N/(2√(μ D₁ D₂))`, which
  is the `μ = 1` value divided by `√μ`.)

* **Off `α = 0` it fails, and it fails for a structural reason.** Split
  `p = p₁ + p₂` with `Δp₁ = −2∂_x v` (linear) and `Δp₂ = −∂_j u_i ∂_i u_j`
  (quadratic). Then `(1−μ)Π` contains `(1−μ)∫p₂ ∂_x u dV`, which is **cubic** in
  `u`. Since the negative terms in (2.3) are quadratic, and the perturbation
  amplitude is a free parameter, a cubic term of either sign cannot be dominated:
  `dV_μ/dt` is guaranteed positive at large amplitude in some direction. A
  quadratic functional other than `E` therefore yields at best a *conditional*
  (finite-amplitude) result, never a global one.

This is the precise obstruction that the quartic functional of §3 is designed to
remove: one needs a term in `V` whose derivative supplies a **negative quartic**
to dominate the cubic, without itself generating a quintic.
